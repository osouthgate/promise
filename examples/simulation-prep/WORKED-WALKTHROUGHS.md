# Two worked Promise conversations

These are **fictional owner answers written for this exercise**, not a transcript,
not Oliver's words and not real authorisation. The local commands that checked
their resulting artifacts are recorded separately in each project's trace.
No language-model eval or autonomous conversational-agent trial ran.

## ReportCheck

### New: what does the person want to be true?

The simulated owner wants teachers to submit CSV reports, a head of year to review
possible mistakes, and each teacher to receive useful feedback on their own reports.
The ownership boundary is explicit: the system flags; the head of year decides.
It prepares output rather than sending student reports automatically.

The simulation asks whether spelling, grammar and copied text are independent jobs.
The fictional owner answers that they are reasons to inspect a report within one
review job, not separate things the head of year sets out to accomplish. That yields
three initial outcome docs and rows: import, review, teacher feedback.

The simulated owner chooses strict fictional rules for teacher separation and
unresolved findings, and confirms the initial worked examples. Those decisions
are stored as exercise inputs; the docs' `agreed` markers do not record a real
human gate. Actual map ids are allocated by the starter reader, and actual lint
and bridge checks verify the initial snapshots and row links.

### Intake: add cases without redefining the job

The fictional owner adds two complaints: common classroom phrases should not be
flagged as copied reports, and a same-student revision needs its earlier version.
The release-note test says these extend review, not a new “so that”. Actual map
assembly appends both cases. Because the review row already has five scenarios,
current Promise requires a continuation row with a `parent` link.

The fictional owner would still describe one review capability. The additional
row is driven by the framework's size limit, not a newly stated user outcome.
The alternative relaxed map deliberately retains all seven scenarios in one job;
the real current checker rejects it. The experiment does not waive that rule.

### Reconcile: the output is wrong

A fixed stub returns false for “review decisions survive into the output”. That
contradicts an existing feedback promise. Actual local lookup and report scripts
retain its existing home; no capability is filed for fixing it. The scripted test
fails as intended. This is a fixture failure, not a discovered application bug,
and switching it back is not a production mutation or a kill witness.

Meanwhile, service checks are independent: fast/high-enough quality can meet the
target, a slower high-quality result can be acceptable, a missing keyboard
assessment is unchecked, and a fixed failed assessment is unacceptable. Passing
functional stubs do not change those service results.

### New: the principal wants a year summary

This actor needs a year-wide overview to direct support, rather than individual
teacher feedback. The fictional owner identifies a new outcome. The simulation
creates a new doc and row, and supplies no result yet. The real report preserves
that result as skipped/unchecked. The prior output regression's fixed stub now
passes, but every stored functional verdict still remains `not-built` because
there is no actual product evidence.

## BorrowWorks

### New: define the user jobs before listing details

The fictional owner wants members to reserve usable equipment, coordinators to
approve restricted loans and account for collections/returns, and maintenance
staff to keep unsafe equipment out of circulation. That yields four jobs.
A cancellation, a collision and an ineligible member are cases of reservation;
quarantine is part of the maintenance outcome. A database field or a test method
gets no capability of its own.

The simulation surfaces cross-site permission and equipment safety choices as
protected fictional product decisions. The fictional owner supplies scoped
answers for the exercise; they confer no real access. Actual local map, lint and
bridge tools process the resulting 16 scenarios and four rows.

### Intake: waiting list, expiry and keyboard booking

The fictional owner explains that all three help a member secure a usable booking.
They extend reservation rather than introduce three separate user outcomes.
The reservation job grows from four to seven cases, requiring one continuation
row under today's rule. Service criteria remain shared policy definitions; they
are not new business capabilities.

A fixed false result in cross-site approval is routed to the existing refusal
case. The stub failure is visible without another row for “deny foreign-site
approval”. In the same stage, the service replay records acceptable slower
responses and an unacceptable fictional keyboard assessment independently.

### Reconcile: repair a case without changing its promise

The next fixed result returns true for that refusal. This simulates repairing
behaviour, not changing the `Then`. No story is reworded and no new refusal
capability is created. There is still no real product test, so the stored verdict
is not promoted just because the synthetic report is lane-green.

### New: the manager wants utilisation information

A manager wants utilisation figures to plan equipment purchases. That is a new
actor outcome rather than another reservation edge case. The simulation creates
its own doc and row with an unchecked result. No purchasing or payment runs.
A general deadline assessment illustrates another service domain without a new
service-type enum; the observation is supplied as a fixed input, not verified
message delivery.

## Shared conclusion

The hard part is deciding whether a new request changes the actor's outcome. The
mechanical checks can confirm shapes, citations and service evidence envelopes;
they cannot independently make that product judgement. The existing job rule
works on these examples. The observed sixth-scenario split, policy-reference
expansion and lane-level proof interpretation are concrete candidates for
improvement, as set out in [the findings](FINDINGS.md).
